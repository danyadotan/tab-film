TAB@WORK · A NOTE FOR THE COO from intention to done

It's 18:00 on a day your people worked with TAB. How does it feel?

You already bought the tools. The question isn't what's installed. It's what changed by Friday. Seven questions worth answering honestly.

1
How wide is the gap between Copilot on every desktop and the usage numbers at the end of the week?
A chat assistant waits to be asked. TAB works inside the flow and meets the employee at the stall. The value doesn't depend on who remembers to prompt.
2
Do you sleep well the night before you report to management?
One anonymous friction ledger shows where work stalled, what it cost and what was treated. Those are numbers you can stand behind.
3
Fewer open ends, and clients who notice?
Every loop ends the day closed, handed off, or on tomorrow's list. Nothing falls between shifts, and clients feel it before the survey does.
4
Impressive new clients who weren't there before?
Hours won back from stalls are hours for clients. The pilot measures how many, so growth is something you can plan, not hope for.
5
How is work–life balance going lately?
Less friction means fewer evenings spent finishing what the day stalled. That's an outcome, not a reading. TAB doesn't monitor how anyone feels.
6
Is IT busy only with the major tickets?
A repeated stall is routed once, to its owner: one ticket, not two hundred. The benchmark to beat is 28% fewer tickets (Nexthink customer outcomes).
7
Has the operational bottleneck moved, or actually shrunk?
The ledger answers week over week: where stalls cluster and whether they're thinning. Detect, quantify, treat, maintain.
THE ONLY MATH
Friction events × the average cost of a stall.
Anchor: $260 per employee per hour of downtime (ITIC 2025).
WHERE IT PAYS MOST
In an RCT of 5,179 support agents (Stanford/MIT), AI help raised output 14% on average and 34% for new and struggling workers, and retention improved.
WHO PAYS, FOR WHOM
Operations and IT pay. It comes from the digital-experience budget, not wellbeing. Frontline and new employees come first; that's where the delta concentrates.
BUILT TO BE ALLOWED
Anonymous by architecture. It counts events, not people, and measures friction, not emotion, which keeps it clear of the EU AI Act's ban on emotion inference at work.
